 Cargo Compartment Halon Replacement

Cargo compartment fire testing is currently in progress as part of the overall Halon Replacement Program of the FAA William J. Hughes Technical Center, Fire Safety Section. The initial goal of the testing is to quantify the current level of protection provided by the Halon 1301 total flood suppressions systems currently used in aircraft Class C cargo compartments. This will be done for several fire threats including a surface burning fire, a deep-seated smoldering fire, and a fire involving the release of hydrocarbon propellant from a burst aerosol can. The testing is conducted in a DC-10 test article cargo compartment with a volume of 2000 cubic feet. The compartment and cabin sections of the test article are extensively instrumented to measure temperature, smoke levels, concentrations of oxygen, carbon monoxide, carbon dioxide, and acid gas decomposition products from different extinguishing agents. The current level of protection will be compared with the results of testing of alternate agents and systems. Some of the agents and systems that have been tested to date include gaseous halocarbons (HFC-227ea and HFC-125), high pressure water mist and pyrotechnically-generated aerosols. The results of the testing are presented at International Halon Replacement Working Group meetings as soon as they are available. The working group members provide input for future testing of particular agents and systems with properties that are desirable to the airlines and aircraft manufacturers. The results of the testing can be used by airlines and aircraft manufacturers to select the systems that will eventually replace the current Halon systems. The results will also be used by the Airworthiness Authorities for the certification of these new systems. Minimum performance standards will be established for the protection of cargo compartments. The program test schedule is flexible and will test promising new agents or systems as they are identified by the Halon Options Task Group.

This task group was formed as part of the FAA sponsored International Halon Replacement Working Group. The purpose is to develop performance standards that can be used to certify cargo compartment suppression systems. The current version of the MPS was last updated in June, 2000. Any comments will be incorporated into a new version of the MPS which will be distributed to the task group members at a future date.
